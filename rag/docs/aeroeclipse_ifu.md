# AeroEclipse Breath-Actuated Nebulizer — Instructions for Use (sample)

## What it is

A breath-actuated nebulizer (BAN) that converts liquid medication into a
fine mist for inhalation. Unlike continuous nebulizers, it only releases
medication while the patient is actively inhaling, which reduces wasted
medication and environmental drug exposure.

## Using the device

1. Wash hands before preparing the device.
2. Fill the medication chamber with the prescribed dose, not exceeding the
   maximum fill line.
3. Attach the mouthpiece (or mask, for young children or anyone unable to
   use a mouthpiece reliably) and connect to the compressor.
4. Turn on the compressor.
5. Put the mouthpiece in the mouth and form a seal with the lips, or fit
   the mask snugly over the nose and mouth.
6. Breathe in slowly and deeply through the mouth — the device releases
   mist automatically during inhalation and stops during exhalation.
7. Continue until the medication chamber is empty or begins to sputter,
   typically 8-10 minutes depending on fill volume.

## Common technique errors

- **Shallow or rapid breathing** — the breath-actuated valve needs a
  reasonably deep, steady inhale to trigger consistently; rapid shallow
  breaths reduce the amount of medication delivered per breath.
- **Breathing through the nose instead of the mouth** (mouthpiece use) —
  bypasses the device entirely; a nose clip can help patients who
  struggle with mouth breathing.
- **Tilting or laying the device on its side during use** — can affect
  medication delivery and, on some units, cause the chamber to leak.
- **Overfilling the medication chamber** — can affect nebulization
  performance and treatment time; always fill to the prescribed volume,
  not the maximum line, unless the maximum line is the prescribed dose.

## Cleaning and maintenance

Rinse the medication chamber and mouthpiece/mask with warm water after
every use and air dry. Perform a deeper wash with mild soap daily during
periods of regular use. Replace the nebulizer set (chamber, mouthpiece,
tubing) per the manufacturer's recommended interval, typically every few
months with regular daily use, since valve wear reduces breath-actuation
reliability over time.

## When to contact a clinician

Contact a healthcare provider if a treatment session is taking noticeably
longer than usual (may indicate a clogged or worn nebulizer), if
breathlessness worsens despite consistent treatments, or if the device
stops actuating reliably with breathing.
