# AeroChamber Valved Holding Chamber — Instructions for Use (sample)

## What it is

A valved holding chamber ("spacer") used with a metered-dose inhaler (MDI)
to slow the medication spray and make it easier to inhale a full dose,
particularly for children, older adults, and anyone who struggles to
coordinate pressing the inhaler with breathing in.

## Priming

Before first use, and if the chamber hasn't been used in more than two
weeks, prime it by spraying the inhaler into the chamber 1-2 times before
the first actual dose. This reduces static charge inside the chamber that
can otherwise cause medication to stick to the walls instead of reaching
the lungs.

## Using the device

1. Remove the inhaler cap and shake the inhaler.
2. Insert the inhaler mouthpiece into the back of the chamber.
3. Put the chamber mouthpiece in the mouth, forming a full seal with the lips.
4. Press the inhaler once to release a dose into the chamber.
5. Breathe in slowly and steadily. A whistle or "yellow flag" indicator
   (on chambers that have one) sounds or moves if the inhale is too fast.
6. Hold the breath for 5-10 seconds if possible, then breathe out.
7. Wait 30-60 seconds between puffs if a second dose is prescribed.

## Common technique errors

- **Inhaling too fast** — triggers the flow-rate whistle/indicator and
  deposits more medication in the throat instead of the lungs.
- **Multiple actuations before inhaling** — pressing the inhaler more than
  once into the chamber before breathing in wastes medication, since the
  first dose partially settles before the second is added.
- **Not forming a full lip seal** — lets medication escape around the
  mouthpiece instead of being inhaled.
- **Long delay between actuation and inhalation** — medication droplets
  settle inside the chamber within a few seconds; inhale promptly after
  pressing the inhaler.

## Cleaning and maintenance

Wash the chamber in warm water with mild dish soap at least once a week.
Do not scrub or towel-dry the inside — air dry to avoid rebuilding static
charge. Replace the chamber approximately every 6-12 months, or sooner if
the valve appears cracked, sticky, or doesn't move freely.

## When to contact a clinician

Contact a healthcare provider if breathlessness, wheeze, or cough is
worsening despite consistent use, or if the device's valve is visibly
damaged and a replacement isn't immediately available.
